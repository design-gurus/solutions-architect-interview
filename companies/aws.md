# AWS Solutions Architect Interview

The AWS solutions architect (SA) interview is commonly reported to have a recruiter call, an optional online assessment, a phone screen, a final loop, and a presentation. AWS does not publish its SA loop, so every detail below comes from preparation guides and candidate reports. The reports agree on one point: behavioral questions based on Amazon's Leadership Principles fill most of the process.

## The reported stages

| Stage | Commonly reported format | What is evaluated |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Recruiter call | A call with a recruiter | Background and fit for the role |
| 2. Online assessment | Optional. One report describes technical and behavioral sections, and another adds a work simulation | Technical knowledge and work judgment |
| 3. Phone screen | One interviewer, mostly technical, with one or two behavioral questions at the end | Breadth on core services |
| 4. Final loop | Five rounds in one report, 4 to 6 interviews in another | Leadership Principles, system design, and depth in the role's area |
| 5. Presentation | 20 minutes plus 10 minutes of questions in one report, 30 minutes in another | Communication and technical judgment |

The reports may describe different teams or older formats. Ask the recruiter which stages apply to the role.

## The Leadership Principles

Amazon's hiring pages say that its interview questions are based on its Leadership Principles. These are a published list of statements that describe how Amazon expects its employees to decide and act. The same pages recommend the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for answers.

One report describes a final loop of five rounds, three of them mostly behavioral. Behavioral questions also close the phone screen and are reported to open the presentation. A candidate who prepares only the technical rounds is unprepared for most of the loop.

**What to do:** prepare six or more stories of about two minutes each, and map every story to two or three principles. The free [Amazon Leadership Principles guide](https://github.com/design-gurus/amazon-leadership-principles) has a page for each principle with questions and a sample answer.

## The two technical rounds

The same report describes two technical rounds.

| Round | What it asks |
|---|---|
| System design | A cloud solution for a customer need, defended while the interviewer changes the requirements |
| Depth in the role's area | Detailed questions in the candidate's specialty |

A generalist SA covers many services for a set of customers. A specialist SA covers one area, such as databases, analytics, or machine learning, so the depth round goes further into that area. Use AWS service names in both rounds, and give a reason for each choice.

## The presentation

The reports disagree on length and audience. One says the round is now one to one with the hiring manager: one or two Leadership Principle questions, 20 minutes of presentation, and 10 minutes of questions. It also says the hiring manager may waive the round. Another report describes a 30-minute presentation with interviewers who act as customers.

A safe plan keeps the core talk at 20 minutes and marks 10 minutes of optional detail. Choose one past project with a clear problem, a rejected option, and a measured result. The [presentation page](../rounds/presentation.md) gives an eight-slide plan.

## The Bar Raiser

A Bar Raiser is an Amazon interviewer from outside the hiring team who takes part in the hiring decision. The reports used for this page do not say whether an SA loop includes one. Ask the recruiter, and prepare every behavioral answer to the same standard in either case.

## A two-week preparation order

1. Days 1 to 4: build the story bank and map it to the principles.
2. Days 5 to 8: practice one design case a day with a cost estimate.
3. Days 9 to 11: build and time the presentation.
4. Days 12 to 14: review core services with three-layer answers from the [technical screen page](../rounds/technical-screen.md).
