# Microsoft Cloud Solution Architect Interview

Microsoft calls the role cloud solution architect, and the job mainly starts after the customer has signed. The loop is therefore likely to ask whether the candidate can turn a signed agreement into running workloads. Microsoft publishes no loop for this role, so the details below come from its careers site and one preparation guide.

## What Microsoft's careers site says

- Most interviews include 2 to 4 conversations with potential teammates and colleagues from other teams.
- Each conversation lasts up to an hour, by phone, on Microsoft Teams, or in person.
- Some roles ask candidates to write code or provide work samples.
- Interviewers look for respect, integrity, accountability, and growth mindset.

**Growth mindset** means treating ability as something that grows through learning. A strong answer therefore shows what the candidate learned from a mistake and what changed afterward. The site also asks for specific examples from past work, not general statements.

## A commonly reported loop

One preparation guide describes four rounds. It names no sources, so the lengths are reports, not policy.

| Round | Reported length | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter screen | About 30 minutes | Background, motivation, and why Microsoft |
| Technical and architecture | 45 to 60 minutes | A design with trade-offs, often on a case or whiteboard |
| Behavioral and customer-facing | 45 to 60 minutes | Past work, with a focus on the customer |
| Hiring manager | 45 to 60 minutes | Fit and business judgment. Some loops add a presentation. |

Four rounds at 30, 60, 60, and 60 minutes total 3.5 hours, which fits the careers site's limit of an hour for each conversation.

## What the job measures

Postings for the role list duties such as "drive consumption growth", leading proofs of concept, and acting as a trusted advisor. **Consumption** is the amount of the platform that a customer actually uses and pays for each month. The architect is measured on whether workloads move and grow after the agreement.

Each phrase in the posting points to one answer to prepare.

| Phrase in the posting | Answer to prepare |
|---|---|
| "Drive consumption growth" | An adoption story with numbers |
| Lead proofs of concept | A proof whose pass criteria the customer agreed before it started |
| "Trusted advisor" | A time you told a customer no and offered a better option |

## A sample adoption story

> "A customer had moved 12 of its 30 workloads after signing, and then the migration stopped. I interviewed the team leads and found that the database team had no training on the managed database service. I ran two workshops and paired with them on the first migration. Within a quarter, 9 more workloads moved, so 21 of 30 were running."

The story names the blocker, the action, and a result that matches how the role is measured.

## What changes with level

Use one question to compare answers: "A customer signed, but usage has not grown in six months. What do you do?"

| Level | A strong answer shows |
|---|---|
| Mid-level | Finds which workloads are blocked and why, then removes one blocker |
| Senior | Also agrees on a migration plan with dates and tracks usage against it each month |
| Principal | Also finds the pattern across several customers and turns it into guidance for other architects |

This table is a preparation guide, not a published standard.

## Questions to ask the recruiter

1. How many conversations are in the loop, and how long is each?
2. Is there a presentation or a case study, and is the topic given in advance?
3. Which track is the team hiring for, such as data and AI or infrastructure?
