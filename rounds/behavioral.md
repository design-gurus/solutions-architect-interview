# The Behavioral Round

Behavioral interviews for a solutions architect (SA) ask about past customers, conflicts, and failures. The stories differ from a software engineer's stories in one way: most of them include a customer, and at least one should include a deal that was lost. At AWS, behavioral questions are commonly reported to fill more than half of the final loop.

## The story families

Prepare about eight stories of two minutes each. Together they should cover these five families.

| Family | Typical question | What the interviewer listens for |
|---|---|---|
| Customers and escalations | "Tell me about a time a customer was unhappy with your work or your product." | You learned the real concern before acting |
| Influence | "Tell me about a time you changed a customer's mind." | Evidence did the persuading, not seniority |
| Conflict | "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a customer or a sales colleague." | You disagreed openly and kept the relationship |
| Failure and lost deals | "Tell me about a deal you lost." | You name your own part first and a real change afterward |
| Saying no | "Tell me about a time you told a customer no." | You offered a better option with the refusal |

## The structure of an answer

Use the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result. An SA answer adds three things to it.

1. **The customer's business goal,** stated as a number or a date.
2. **Your own decision,** separated from what the account team did.
3. **A measured result,** followed by what you now do differently.

Expect follow-up questions that ask for your own decisions and your own numbers. An answer that says "we" throughout gives the interviewer nothing to score.

## A weak and a strong answer

**Question:** "Tell me about a deal you lost."

> **Weak:** "We lost a large retail customer to a competitor because their price was lower. There was not much we could do about the price."

> **Strong:** "A logistics company was choosing a platform for 40 warehouse applications. I ran the proof of concept and tested the 3 applications that I judged hardest. I never asked who approved the spending. Their finance lead joined in week 6, saw no yearly cost comparison, and chose the competitor, who had provided one. My mistake was treating the engineers as the only audience. Since then, I ask in the first call who signs and what each approver needs to see. In my next 5 proofs of concept, the cost comparison was in the first proposal, and 4 of them closed."

The strong answer names the mistake in one sentence, gives numbers, and ends with a changed habit and its result.

## Company frameworks

| Company | What to prepare |
|---|---|
| AWS | Amazon's hiring pages say its interviews are based on its Leadership Principles. Map every story to two or three principles. The free [Amazon Leadership Principles guide](https://github.com/design-gurus/amazon-leadership-principles) covers each one. |
| Microsoft | Its careers site names respect, integrity, accountability, and growth mindset. Growth mindset means treating ability as something that grows through learning, so each failure story needs what changed afterward. |
| Databricks | Its interview guide says the hiring manager asks for examples of deals won and lost. |
