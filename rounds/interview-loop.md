# The Solutions Architect Interview Loop

Most solutions architect (SA) loops start with a recruiter screen and one or two technical or hiring manager screens. They end with several final interviews and, at many companies, a presentation. Only Databricks publishes its full process, so most other details come from candidate reports and preparation guides.

## The stages in order

| Stage | Commonly reported format | What is evaluated |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Recruiter screen | A call of about 30 minutes | Background, motivation, and why this company |
| 2. Online assessment (some companies) | A timed test | Technical knowledge and work judgment |
| 3. Hiring manager screen | About 1 hour | Customer results, including deals won and lost |
| 4. Technical screen | About 1 hour with one interviewer | Breadth across cloud, data, and the vendor's products |
| 5. Coding assignment (some companies) | Practical code, not puzzles | Data handling and feature work |
| 6. Architecture design | Whiteboard or shared drawing | Trade-offs, cost, and changing requirements |
| 7. Customer role-play | The interviewer plays a customer | Discovery and objection handling |
| 8. Presentation | 20 to 60 minutes | Structure and answers to questions |
| 9. Behavioral | One to three interviews | Past decisions, conflict, and customer trust |

No company runs all nine stages. A typical loop uses five or six of them.

## How loops differ by company type

**Cloud providers.** The loops at AWS, Microsoft, and Google Cloud test breadth across the platform and a design for a customer need. AWS is commonly reported to spend more than half of its final loop on behavioral questions. Google Cloud calls the role customer engineer, and Microsoft calls it cloud solution architect.

**Data platforms.** Databricks and Snowflake add practical work. Databricks lists a coding assignment on data frame operations, and Snowflake candidates report a take-home task. Both loops end in a presentation, and Snowflake's is reported to include a live demonstration.

**AI companies.** Some AI companies post the role as applied AI architect. They publish no loop, and public reports are few. The postings stress discovery, prototypes, evaluations, and example code that customers can extend.

## Which round carries the most weight

The answer depends on the company, but one pattern holds across the reports. The rounds that a software engineer loop does not have, the customer role-play and the presentation, are where engineers moving into the role score lowest. An engineer who prepares only the design round has prepared about one third of the loop.

A useful first estimate of preparation time is below. Adjust it after the recruiter confirms the loop.

| Round | Share of preparation time |
|---|---:|
| Architecture design, with cost | 30 percent |
| Presentation | 20 percent |
| Customer role-play | 20 percent |
| Behavioral stories | 20 percent |
| Technical screen | 10 percent |

## Five questions to ask the recruiter

Because most loops are unpublished, the recruiter is the best source. Ask these questions on the first call:

1. Which rounds are in the loop, and how long is each one?
2. Is there a presentation, who attends it, and who chooses the topic?
3. Is there a coding assignment or an online assessment?
4. Is the role mainly before the sale or after it?
5. Is the role generalist, or does it specialize in an area such as data or security?
